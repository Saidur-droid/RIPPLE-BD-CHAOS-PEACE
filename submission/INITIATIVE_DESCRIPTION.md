# Initiative Description — working draft (under 500 words)

**RIPPLE BD — CHAOS → PEACE** is a mobile-first digital peace simulator designed for young people in Bangladesh. Its premise is simple: responsible digital behaviour is learned more deeply when people can experience the consequence of a decision, reverse it, practise a safer response, and see what changed.

Inside a fictional digital community called **Nodi**, users face three Bangladesh-relevant scenarios: a cropped viral clip that can inflame interfaith tension, a meme that develops into gendered online harassment, and a sweeping claim about a fictional minority community where the people being discussed are missing from the conversation. Users first make a natural choice without being told the “correct” answer. RIPPLE’s deterministic simulation then makes the social and human consequences visible through changes in reach, hostility, safety and community trust. These numbers are clearly labelled as simulation indicators, not real-world causal estimates.

The signature **REWIND** moment gives users the same situation again with safer tools. They can verify sources, add context, de-escalate discussion, support a targeted person, preserve necessary evidence, report harm, or seek relevant first-person sources. **Manipulation X-Ray** reveals cues such as urgency, cropped context, social proof, vague authority and identity framing. **SHIELD Mode** teaches a practical five-step bystander sequence: Recognize, Support, Preserve, Report and Escalate.

RIPPLE BD is intentionally feasible. The prototype uses React, TypeScript and local scenario data; it requires no account, database, paid API, AI service or sensitive personal data. It can be hosted through GitHub Pages at essentially zero software cost, making it realistic for youth clubs, universities and community sessions with limited resources.

Our pre-submission pilot is designed to test usability and immediate learning with a small convenience sample of local youth. We will compare anonymous baseline and post-experience Digital Peace Decision Scores across verification, manipulation recognition, bystander response, and support/de-escalation. We will report real participant numbers, completion, confusion points and product changes transparently, without claiming causal impact from a small pilot.

By February 2027, our target is for at least 200 young people in our target division to complete RIPPLE BD, with an average scenario-based DPDS improvement of at least 20 percentage points and follow-up participants retaining at least a 10-point improvement after seven days.
