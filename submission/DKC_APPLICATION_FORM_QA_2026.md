## 4. What specific problem are you trying to solve?

**Form guidance:** 150-200 words.

**Answer:**

Every click has a consequence, but most young people are never given a safe place to practise what to do before that click.

In Dhaka, young people move through fast online spaces where misinformation, cyberbullying, harassment, gendered abuse and exclusion can spread through shares, comments and group behaviour. The problem is not simply lack of awareness. Many already know messages like "think before you share," yet real decisions happen under pressure, emotion, social proof and fear of missing out.

One harmful choice can amplify a false claim, expose a target to further harassment, encourage bystanders to join the pile-on, or weaken trust between people and communities. Passive awareness content often explains the right behaviour after the fact, but does not let users practise the decision itself.

RIPPLE BD focuses on this gap between knowing and doing. We want to help youth build safer digital reflexes: pause, verify, avoid amplifying harm, support people responsibly, de-escalate conflict and make more thoughtful choices online.

## 5. What is your proposed solution?

**Form guidance:** 250 words or less.

**Answer:**

Our proposed solution is **RIPPLE BD**, a mobile-first Digital Peace Simulator built to turn awareness into practice.

Instead of telling young people what the "correct" answer is, RIPPLE places them inside short, fictional, Bangladesh-relevant online scenarios. Through **MIRROR**, the user first makes a natural choice. The **Ripple Engine** then shows how that choice can affect reach, reactions, a fictional person and wider community trust. The user can **REWIND** the moment, choose a safer response through **PEACE**, and immediately see how a different action can change the direction of the situation.

The experience also includes **Manipulation X-Ray**, which helps users notice cues such as emotional urgency, cropped context, missing sources and social proof. In targeted-harm scenarios, **SHIELD Mode** guides users through practical bystander steps: Recognize, Support, Preserve, Report and Escalate. A **Digital Reflex Profile** then reflects decision quality across verification, manipulation recognition, bystander response, support and de-escalation.

A working Bangla and English MVP already exists. During the fellowship, we plan to refine the experience with youth in Dhaka, run small facilitated Ripple Labs, test baseline and post-experience decisions, use short follow-up scenarios, and improve the product based on what participants actually struggle with.

## 6. Beyond community activities, what other solution(s) will your team bring?

**Answer:**

Our aim is not to run one campaign and disappear. We want to leave behind a practical digital learning tool that can be used, tested and improved repeatedly.

RIPPLE BD is designed as a reusable mobile experience that can work without paid APIs, user accounts or complex infrastructure. Alongside community sessions, we plan to build a stronger library of fictional Bangladesh-relevant scenarios, a simple facilitation guide for schools, campuses and youth groups, and a measurement framework that looks at decision quality, not only attendance or self-reported awareness.

The longer-term idea is simple: a young person should be able to practise a difficult online decision before facing a similar moment in real life. Short follow-up scenarios can help us see whether safer choices are retained, while feedback can guide which scenarios need improvement.

With DKC mentorship, safeguarding guidance and community testing, RIPPLE BD has the potential to grow from a working MVP into a locally grounded digital peace learning resource that others can adapt responsibly. The core remains simple: **experience the consequence, rewind the moment, practise a safer response, reflect, and try again.**

## 7. What is your community engagement plan?

**Answer:**

We want young people to shape RIPPLE BD, not simply receive it.

Our community engagement will begin with small **Ripple Labs** in Dhaka, working with university and college students, youth clubs and community youth networks. Each session will combine the mobile simulator with guided reflection, SHIELD practice and short discussions around real online decision-making.

Participants will not only use the product. They will help improve it. We will ask which scenarios feel realistic, which language feels natural in Bangla or Banglish, where users feel confused, and what safer responses actually feel practical in their everyday digital lives. This feedback will guide scenario revisions and product improvements.

We also plan to use simple pre and post decision exercises to understand whether participants are making safer choices after the experience, followed by short Day 7 scenarios where possible.

Our goal is to create a continuous loop: **reach youth, let them experience RIPPLE, listen carefully, improve the product, and return with a better version.** This keeps the initiative locally grounded instead of becoming a one-way awareness campaign.

## 9. What is your digital media campaign plan & strategy?

**Form guidance:** 250 words or less.

**Answer:**

Our digital campaign, **#EveryClickRipples**, will take the central idea of RIPPLE BD beyond facilitated sessions: small online choices can create larger consequences.

The primary audience will be young people aged 18 to 35, initially focused on Dhaka. We plan to use platforms where youth already spend time, particularly **Facebook, Instagram and short-form video platforms**, alongside campus and youth-community pages where access is available.

Rather than posting generic awareness messages, our content will invite participation. Examples include short "What would you do?" scenarios, cropped-context challenges, verification prompts, bystander-response choices, Manipulation X-Ray explainers and short before-and-after decision stories inspired by the RIPPLE learning loop.

Each piece of content will connect to one practical behaviour such as **pause, verify, support, de-escalate or avoid amplifying harm**. Selected posts can direct people to the mobile experience through a QR code or link.

We will also turn learning from Ripple Labs into campaign content without exposing participant identities or personal experiences. No real victim stories or sensitive personal data will be used.

The campaign will follow the same principle as the product: **do not tell people what to think. Give them a moment to think before they click.**

**Campaign line:**  
**One click creates a ripple. Make yours count. #EveryClickRipples**

## 10. How will you sustain your solution?

**Form guidance:** 200 words or less.

**Answer:**

RIPPLE BD is designed to continue beyond the Fellowship because the core product is lightweight, reusable and low-cost to operate.

The current MVP runs as a mobile-first web experience and does not depend on paid APIs, user accounts or expensive infrastructure. This allows us to keep improving the experience without creating a large recurring software cost.

Our sustainability model has three parts. First, we will keep expanding and refining the fictional scenario library based on participant feedback and safeguarding review. Second, we will create simple Ripple Lab facilitation materials so youth clubs, campuses and community facilitators can run guided sessions without needing the core team every time. Third, we will continue using short follow-up scenarios and the Digital Peace Decision Score to understand what participants are learning and where the product needs improvement.

If the approach proves useful, we hope to gradually adapt it for more youth settings and different online-harm contexts.

Our long-term goal is modest but practical: **build a tool that becomes easier to reuse, improve and share each time it is tested.**
