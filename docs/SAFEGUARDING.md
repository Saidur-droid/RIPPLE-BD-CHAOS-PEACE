# Safeguarding & Ethical Design

RIPPLE works with sensitive topics. Product quality includes **not causing avoidable harm**.

## 1. Fiction-first

All MVP scenarios are fictional composites.

Avoid:
- real active incidents,
- identifiable victims,
- real perpetrators,
- current political campaigns,
- fabricated allegations about real religious or ethnic groups.

## 2. No victim-story harvesting

The MVP must never ask:
“Tell us what happened to you.”

We can evaluate learning without collecting trauma histories.

## 3. SHIELD Mode boundary

SHIELD Mode is an educational simulation.

It does not:
- provide emergency response,
- investigate cases,
- reveal identities,
- promise legal outcomes,
- replace police/platform/professional support.

## 4. Evidence-preservation education

If teaching evidence preservation:
- preserve only what is necessary,
- do not encourage redistribution of abusive material,
- do not store real sensitive images in RIPPLE,
- avoid screenshots containing unnecessary personal information.

## 5. Real-world escalation copy

Use wording such as:
“If there is an immediate safety risk, contact an appropriate trusted person or official service.”

Do not claim partnership with any service unless confirmed.

## 6. Community representation

For minority/indigenous scenarios:
- avoid speaking “for” a real community,
- use fictionalized composites for MVP,
- if a real community is later featured, co-design and obtain informed consent,
- give communities control over how their stories are represented.

## 7. Gender-based harm

Avoid:
- graphic content,
- victim blaming,
- implying public confrontation is always safest,
- implying that one reporting action guarantees resolution.

## 8. Participant privacy

Pre-submission pilot:
- no names required,
- no sensitive identity fields,
- random participant code,
- only aggregated reporting.

## 9. Content review gate

Every scenario must pass:
- factual-risk review,
- stereotype review,
- trauma/sensitivity review,
- language review,
- “could this be mistaken for a real allegation?” check.

## 10. Design principle

> The simulator should create reflection, not shock for shock's sake.
