# Bangladesh Evidence Brief — judge-safe facts

> Purpose: verified context for the product, deck and speaking notes. These are external statistics, not RIPPLE pilot results.

## 1. Misinformation is a major social-media stressor for young people

UNICEF Bangladesh reported on 11 February 2025 that **two in three** respondents in an anonymous U-Report poll of children and young people said “too much fake news and misinformation” was the most significant cause of stress on social media. **Almost 29,000** people responded.

Use in deck:
> “In a 2025 UNICEF Bangladesh U-Report poll with almost 29,000 respondents, two in three young people identified too much fake news and misinformation as their biggest social-media stressor.”

Source:
https://www.unicef.org/bangladesh/en/press-releases/unicef-youth-poll-misinformation-leading-cause-stress-youth-social-media

Caution:
- U-Report is an opt-in poll, not a nationally representative prevalence survey.
- Say “respondents,” not “all Bangladeshi youth.”

## 2. Online bullying is a documented Bangladesh child-safety problem

A UNICEF-commissioned Bangladesh study of **1,281 internet-using children aged 10–17** reported that **32%** had experienced online bullying related to appearance, examination results, religion or other reasons.

Use in deck:
> “A UNICEF-commissioned study of 1,281 internet-using children aged 10–17 in Bangladesh reported online bullying among 32% of respondents.”

Source:
https://www.unicef.org/bangladesh/en/press-releases/one-million-school-children-receive-online-safety-certification-next-year

Caution:
- The study was reported in 2019.
- Do not present it as a 2026 prevalence estimate.

## 3. Digital harassment can change offline behavior

UN Women’s Bangladesh storytelling report published in June 2026 describes digital harassment as affecting women’s daily movement, clothing choices and sense of offline safety. The report also says that a recent UN Women study found **66% of women surveyed had received explicit or threatening messages on social media**; the article identifies that study as unpublished.

Use in speaking notes:
> “The human consequence is not confined to a screen: UN Women’s 2026 Bangladesh reporting documents how digital threats can change where women go, what they wear, and how safe they feel offline.”

Source:
https://open.unwomen.org/en/story/addressing-digital-violence-through-storytelling-bangladesh

Caution:
- Prefer the documented behavioral-impact description.
- If citing the 66% figure, explicitly say it comes from an unpublished UN Women study cited by UN Women; do not overstate representativeness.

## Evidence rule

External numbers establish the **problem context**.
RIPPLE’s own effectiveness claims must come only from RIPPLE’s real pilot and later evaluation.

Never turn simulation indicators (reach, hostility, safety, trust) into real-world causal effect sizes.
