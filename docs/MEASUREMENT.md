# Measurement Framework

## Objective

Measure **decision quality in simulated digital-peace scenarios**, not attitudes alone.

## Primary metric

# Digital Peace Decision Score (DPDS)

100-point composite:
- Verification / responsible sharing — 25
- Manipulation recognition — 25
- Safe bystander response — 25
- Support + de-escalation — 25

## Proposed fellowship outcome

> By February 2027, at least 200 young people in the target division will complete RIPPLE BD, with the average scenario-based Digital Peace Decision Score improving by at least 20 percentage points from baseline, and follow-up participants retaining at least a 10-point improvement after seven days.

This is a **target**, not a current result.

## Pre-submission pilot metrics

Collect:
- N invited
- N started
- N completed
- completion rate
- baseline DPDS
- immediate post DPDS
- per-dimension change
- median completion time
- top confusion point
- self-reported usefulness
- optional anonymous quote

## Retention

Day 7:
- short unseen scenario,
- same scoring dimensions,
- compare against baseline and immediate post.

## Analysis rules

Do not:
- claim causality from a tiny convenience sample,
- claim representativeness,
- hide dropouts,
- selectively report only favorable questions.

Do:
- report N for every chart,
- report missing follow-up count,
- keep raw calculations reproducible,
- distinguish target values from observed values.

## Pilot decision gate

The MVP is not “validated” because users like it.

We look for:
1. users understand the consequence chain,
2. the same user makes safer decisions after the rewind,
3. post-game scores improve on unseen/parallel items,
4. users can explain one learning point unaided,
5. major confusion or safeguarding issues are absent.

## Example reporting language

Good:
> In a preliminary convenience pilot of N=24 youth, mean DPDS increased from X to Y immediately after the experience. Because this was a small uncontrolled pilot, the result is preliminary.

Bad:
> RIPPLE reduces misinformation by 40%.

## Data minimization

Use random participant ID.
Do not collect sensitive beliefs, political preferences, religion, ethnicity, victim history, or personal harassment details unless a later ethically reviewed study specifically requires them.
