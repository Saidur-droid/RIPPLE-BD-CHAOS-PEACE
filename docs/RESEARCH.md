## 2. Why serious interactive learning is plausible

### Bad News
The Bad News game uses active inoculation: players encounter and practise recognizing manipulation techniques rather than only receiving passive warnings.

Cambridge has reported large-scale public reach and research indicating improved recognition of misinformation after play.

Reference:
- https://www.cam.ac.uk/public-engagement/cambridge-awards/2024/established-academic

### Harmony Square
A randomized controlled study of the Harmony Square game reported that participants exposed to the game rated manipulative social-media content as less reliable and showed reduced willingness to share it.

Reference:
- https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/breaking-harmony-square-a-game-that-inoculates-against-political-misinformation/

### Prebunking videos
Jigsaw/Cambridge research has tested short inoculation-style videos at very large scale, supporting the idea that brief interventions can improve recognition of manipulation techniques.

Reference:
- https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/inoculateexperiment

## 3. Why retention testing matters

Immediate post-game performance is not enough. A later longitudinal study of the Bad News intervention found that some immediate effects weakened over time, reinforcing the importance of memory and booster design.

RIPPLE response:
- baseline,
- immediate post,
- Day-3 micro-booster,
- Day-7 retention test.

Reference:
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-57205-x

## 4. Why SHIELD Mode is plausible

Right To Be's bystander intervention work demonstrates the value of structured, practical response skills. Its 2024 impact report states:
- 3.2 million people trained in its 5D methodology,
- 10,300 community actions against hate,
- 32,657 stories shared/mapped.

Reference:
- https://righttobe.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024_impactreport_rtb.pdf

RIPPLE does **not** copy the 5D model. The evidence supports the broader idea that people can be trained in concrete bystander actions.

## 5. Bangladesh relevance

A 2026 UN Bangladesh / UN Women release, citing the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics 2024 National Violence Against Women Prevalence Survey, states that **16% of women aged 20–24 experienced technology-facilitated gender-based violence in the previous year**.

Reference:
- https://bangladesh.un.org/en/321919-university-grants-commission-and-un-women-partner-end-sexual-and-gender-based-violence

This supports the relevance of including gendered online-harm scenarios, while avoiding overclaiming that the statistic represents all youth or all online harms.

## 6. Evidence rules for this project

We will:
- distinguish global study findings from RIPPLE results;
- report exact sample size for our pilot;
- avoid causality claims from uncontrolled pilot data;
- never invent percentages, testimonials, or partner endorsements;
- label all pilot findings as preliminary;
- publish limitations alongside improvements.

## 7. Research hypothesis

**H1:** A consequence-based, rewind-and-retry simulator can improve scenario-based digital peace decisions immediately after use.

**H2:** Short boosters can help retain part of that improvement after seven days.

**H3:** A combined prevention + bystander-response experience is more useful to participants than passive awareness content alone.

These are hypotheses to test, not facts.
